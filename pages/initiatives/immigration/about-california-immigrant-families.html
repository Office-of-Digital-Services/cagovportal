Immigrants are an important part of communities across California. They contribute to the state’s diverse cultures, communities, and economy. Millions of Californians are immigrants or have immigrant family members, with many different backgrounds, experiences, and immigration statuses.

Immigrant families have different immigration and citizenship statuses

Immigrants have many different immigration statuses. More than half are U.S. citizens. Others are lawful permanent residents (green card holders), visa holders, refugees, or have temporary protections such as Temporary Protected Status (TPS) or Deferred Action for Childhood Arrivals (DACA). Some Californians are undocumented.

Immigrants are part of California families

About half of California children have at least one immigrant parent, and one in five children lives in a mixed-status family. A mixed-status family includes people with different citizenship or immigration statuses, for example, a U.S. citizen child and a parent or grandparent with a different immigration status.

Immigrants are workers and business owners

Immigrants contribute their skills and experience across California’s economy. They make up nearly 40% of the state’s entrepreneurs, 40% of STEM workers, and 34% of nurses. Immigrants also play an important role in agriculture, manufacturing, construction, service industries, and many other sectors.

Immigrants contribute to California’s economy

Immigrant Californians help strengthen communities and the state’s economy through their work, businesses, taxes, and spending. In 2021, immigrants contributed an estimated $51.4 billion in state and local taxes and $81.8 billion in federal taxes, with approximately $354 billion in spending power. Immigrant workers are also essential to many of California’s key industries and communities. Significant losses to the immigrant workforce could disrupt businesses and critical industries, delay construction and rebuilding projects, reduce economic activity, and increase costs for California families and businesses.

Changes in immigration policy and increased immigration enforcement can affect entire families

Federal immigration policies and protections can change. California offers resources to help families understand their rights and connect with legal, health, education, and other services. If you have questions about your immigration status or how a change in federal policy may affect you or your family, seek help from a qualified immigration legal services provider. The resources below can help you find trusted legal services and avoid immigration scams.

State of California’s Council on Immigrant Integration and Talent (Council)

The Council was created by Governor Newsom and the California Legislature in 2022. It builds on the creation of the Governor’s Director of Immigrant Integration in 2015 to further immigrant integration through statewide coordination, evaluation, and technical assistance across the administration. The Council advances immigrant integration and inclusion; leverages the diverse talents and assets of immigrant communities, helping more families secure good, family-supporting jobs; and supports innovation and economic growth for the benefit of all Californians.
